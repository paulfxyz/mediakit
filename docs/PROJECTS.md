## How to read the portfolio

The categories matter more than a total count. Openline is my main business; the consulting and advisory work has a different role; Enki is a public-good direction; and the independent tools are projects with their own scopes and constraints.

I am interested in the practical space between an idea and something somebody can use. That does not require every experiment to become a company, every tool to use AI, or every public repository to make a sweeping claim about the future.

The [life-and-work account](LIFE-AND-WORK.md) provides the personal background, including the places I have lived. This page is the more useful reference when the question is what a project does and how it fits into the wider picture.

### Different problems, not one interchangeable set of apps

- **Capture:** Junk is a deliberately focused scratchpad, built around making a note without turning that moment into a larger workflow ([Junk](https://github.com/paulfxyz/junk)).
- **Operational visibility:** ASE centres on monitoring services, domain records, and certificates, rather than being a general-purpose security guarantee ([ASE](https://github.com/paulfxyz/ase)).
- **Working with models:** Mang, Mercury, and Clippy apply AI to different tasks: terminal commands, research and debate, and contract analysis respectively ([Mang](https://github.com/paulfxyz/mang), [Mercury](https://github.com/paulfxyz/mercury), [Clippy](https://github.com/paulfxyz/clippy)).
- **Communication:** Hollr explores encrypted, timed messages with a shareable point of contact ([Hollr](https://github.com/paulfxyz/hollr)).
- **Information:** Cup of News applies an AI-assisted workflow to news briefings, with an important distinction between its public open-source core and its commercial product ([Cup of News](https://github.com/paulfxyz/cup-of-news)).

That variety is the point of the collection. A scratchpad, an operational monitor, and a contract-analysis tool should be judged by different standards, not by whether they all fit the same launch-day slogan.

The common responsibility is what happens after the demonstration: checking the output, documenting limits, handling failures, and deciding what is worth maintaining. In particular, a model-generated command or analysis should not be treated as safe or correct merely because several models produced it.

## A useful way to cover a project
