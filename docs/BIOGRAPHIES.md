## Extended biography

> Paul Fleury is a French internet entrepreneur and consultant based in Lisbon, working across connectivity, cybersecurity, AI, and digital products ([professional profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/paulfxyz/)). His main business is Openline, where he serves as founder and CEO; his wider work includes Megastack, Calliope Agency, and Asymptote Ventures ([professional profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/paulfxyz/)).
>
> Over the past 15 years, he has lived for at least six months in each of France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Germany, Mexico, the United States, the United Kingdom, Australia, Thailand, Spain, Italy, and Portugal, listed in no particular order. He describes every one of those places as special and enriching in its own way.
>
> Earlier in his career, he spent more than a decade working on confidential defence and security contracts as an external contractor ([professional profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/paulfxyz/)). His current public work focuses on independent entrepreneurship, consulting, and practical software ([professional profile](https://www.linkedin.com/in/paulfxyz/)).
>
> His portfolio ranges from small tools such as Junk and Mang to Cup of News, an AI-assisted news product with a separately licensed open-source core ([Junk](https://github.com/paulfxyz/junk), [Mang](https://github.com/paulfxyz/mang), [Cup of News](https://github.com/paulfxyz/cup-of-news)). He also initiated Enki, a public-good project concerned with decentralised, local-first AI ([Enki](https://enki.ngo)). The Weekathon connects his independent software projects through a public experiment in AI-assisted building, ownership, testing, and improvement after release ([Weekathon](https://ph.paulfleury.com)).
>
> He approaches the portfolio as several different kinds of work: a company, client services, a public-good initiative, and smaller experiments. Useful work is at the centre of his deliberately selective public presence.
